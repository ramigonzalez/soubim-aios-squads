# Story 12.15 — Invite an External Person to a Project

**Story ID:** 12.15
**Epic:** E12 — Organizations & Client Access
**Priority:** High | **Effort:** 5 SP (estimate)
**Status:** Ready for Review
**Type:** Feature (authorization)
**Requested by:** Rami (2026-10-15), replacing "search organizations" in the project sharing section: "I want Owner A to invite someone outside of his organization."

---

## Story

As an **owner or admin of Organization A**, I want to invite any person by e-mail to one of A's projects, so that clients, consultants and partners can follow it without seeing the rest of A.

## Decisions (Rami, 2026-10-15)

1. The invite is by **e-mail**, to anyone, with or without an account.
2. The project **stays Organization A's**. The invited person gets **external access** to that one project. Their own organization, if they have one, gets nothing.
3. An external person sees **only the projects they were invited to**, never the rest of A.
4. The default access is **Viewer**. A's owners/admins can change it to **Contributor** later, still with limited access (below).
5. **Everyone sees that the person is external** (an "Externo" badge wherever the person appears).
6. Until e-mail is sent (12.14 Resend), the owner/admin **copies the invite link** and sends it themselves.
7. (2026-10-15) An external person **always sees what they added themselves** (manual items, meetings they uploaded or imported) while they still have access to the project. Nothing else becomes visible.
8. (2026-10-15) Items a **Contributor** guest adds start **pending** (12.6): A reviews them before they go live. The author sees their own pending item, marked pending; other guests and shared organizations do not.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Invite
- [x] Project settings get a new section, **"Pessoas externas"**, for owners/admins of the owning organization (pt-BR + en). It has:
  - an e-mail input;
  - an "Convidar" button;
  - after inviting, the **link to copy**, until 12.14 sends it;
  - the list of external people and pending invites: name/e-mail, access (Viewer/Contributor), change access, remove, revoke a pending invite.
- [x] Inviting an e-mail that is already a regular member of A answers 409 `already_member`. Inviting someone who is already external on the project answers 409 `already_invited`.
- [x] Invitations reuse 12.5: hashed token, expiry, revoke, one pending invite per e-mail and project.

### Accept
- [x] **Logged in, or with an account:** the link shows "Organization A invites you to project X", with Accept and Reject. The person must be logged in as the invited e-mail (12.5 rule).
- [x] **No account:** the link opens sign-up (name and password, or Google 12.8). Then the same Accept/Reject. Sign-up does **not** force creating an organization (see Assumptions).
- [x] Accepting:
  - makes the person an `external` member of Organization A;
  - assigns them to the project with access `viewer`.
- [x] Rejecting closes the invite. Organization A sees it as "recusado".

### What an external person can do
- [x] They see **only** projects they are assigned to; never A's other projects, member list, settings, Drive, Fathom or sharing.
- [x] **Viewer:** reads what a shared organization sees (12.3/12.4): `shared` meetings only, never `internal`; items per the 12.6 rule for shared organizations.
- [x] **Contributor:** Viewer, plus adding content the way a shared contributor organization can (12.3). They cannot review, manage, share or invite.
- [x] **Own contributions (decision 7):** the author sees their own items and meetings on projects where they still have external access; items extracted from a meeting they did not add, and other guests' content, stay hidden; removal cuts everything.
- [x] **Contributor items are pending (decision 8):** visible to the author (marked pending) and to A's reviewers; approved by A, they then follow the normal rules.
- [x] The organization switcher (12.5) shows A for them with the "Externo" badge. Their own organization, if any, works as before.

### Everyone sees it
- [x] An **"Externo" badge** appears:
  - in A's team page;
  - in the project's people list;
  - next to their name on items and meetings they add.

### Removal
- [x] Removing them from their last project removes the `external` membership. Content they added stays.

### Tests
- [x] Isolation:
  - an external person on project X gets 403/404 on A's project Y, on A's settings and on internal meetings of X;
  - their list endpoints never include Y or internal content.
- [x] Viewer cannot write; Contributor can add content but cannot review, manage or share.
- [x] Accept with an existing account, accept after sign-up, reject, expired, revoked, wrong e-mail logged in.
- [x] Frontend: the "Pessoas externas" section and the accept page.

## Assumptions & gaps (dev, 2026-10-15)

- **Data model:** `organization_members.role = 'external'` (gives the badge and the switcher entry) plus a `project_members` row for each project. `project_members` needs a new `access` column (`viewer`/`contributor`). `external` joins `ASSIGNED_ROLES`, so `accessible_projects_filter` already limits it to assigned projects. The work is in the **content filters** (12.4 visibility, 12.6 review): they must treat `external` like a shared organization, not like A's own member. That is the security-critical part; it gets an Opus review.
- **Sign-up without an organization:** you said the person "must create an Organization if they don't belong to one, or be part of the Organization". Accepting already makes them part of A (as external), so creating their own organization is **optional** and can be done later. Confirmed by Rami (2026-10-15).
- **Existing org-to-org sharing** ("Organizações com acesso", typing the slug) stays as it is. Confirmed by Rami (2026-10-15).
- **E-mail:** copy the link until 12.14.

### Implementation decisions (dev, 12.15)
- **`external` is NOT in `ASSIGNED_ROLES`** (deviation from the assumption above, on purpose). Adding it would make the owning organization appear in `project_access_by_organization` / `_org_grants_access` / `review_visible_filter` for the external person, i.e. they would see A's internal meetings, source-less items and pending items. Instead: `access.external_access_level()` gives `read` / `write` from `project_members.access`, `project_access_level` takes the best of the organization levels and that one, and the owning organization is **never** a key of the per-organization map for an external person. Every rule keyed on "reaches the project through organization X" (12.4 internal content, 12.6 pending/rejected, visibility change, review, `reaches_through_owner`, Fathom routing candidates) leaves them out automatically. SQL side: `accessible_projects_filter` gets one extra branch (`external_project_ids`); nothing else grants them anything. Net effect: exactly a shared organization (viewer → read, contributor → write).
- **Invitation model:** reused `organization_invitations` (12.5) with two new columns: `project_id` (project invitation, role `external`) and `rejected_at`. Alternative was a new table; rejected because token / expiry / single-use claim / revoke / Google sign-up would have to be duplicated. One live (pending or rejected) invitation per e-mail + project; a new invitation revokes the previous one (same rule as 12.5). Organization-level invitation lists ignore project invitations.
- **Accept:** reuses 12.5 (`/invitations/accept` logged in as the invited e-mail; `/invitations/public/accept` sign-up with name + password; Google 12.8 goes through the same functions). Creates `organization_members(role='external')` (unless already external for another project) and `project_members(access='viewer')`. A user who became a regular member of A meanwhile gets 409 `already_member`. No organization is created.
- **Reject:** `POST /invitations/public/reject` with the token, no login needed (holding the link identifies the invitee, as for the sign-up accept). Only project invitations can be rejected (organization invitations: 404). A sees it as "Recusado" until they revoke it ("Remover da lista") or invite again.
- **What a contributor adds** is owned by A (`acting_organization_id` returns A) and internal to A, like a shared organization's member (they cannot create `shared` meetings: `can_create_shared` needs owner/admin). Rami's decisions 7 and 8 (below) add the author exception and the review.
- **Author exception (decision 7)** — `access._own_external_filter` (SQL) / `access._own_external` (single object): `created_by == user` **and** the project is in `external_project_ids(user)` (current external grant on a usable organization). Applied in `visible_sources_filter` (meetings), the source-less branch of `visible_items_filter` (manual items), `review_visible_filter` (own pending / rejected items), `source_visible` (meeting viewer, transcript, recording link, extraction versions, curation), `item_visible` (item detail / edit, V1 decisions) and `owned_object_visible` (in-flight Fathom import, via `fathom_imports.user_id`). Items extracted from the author's own meeting follow that meeting (visible once approved); extracted items have no `created_by`, so pending ones stay with A's reviewers. A guest who also belongs to a shared organization acts for that organization (12.3 rules), not as external.
- **Pending (decision 8)** — `POST /projects/{id}/items` sets `review_status = 'pending'` when the creator reaches the project only as an external person (`project_capabilities.is_external`). Meetings a contributor uploads already go through the ingestion review (source `pending`) and their extracted items already start `pending` (12.6 extraction), so nothing changed there. Once approved, a manual item is still internal to A (source-less items have no visibility switch): other guests and shared organizations never see it, the author and A do.
- **Ingestion queue:** the author's own meeting does not appear in the guest's queue while pending (guests see meetings past review only, security review); it is reachable from its link / the meeting viewer.
- **Author badge:** new nullable `created_by` on `sources` and `project_items` (set by manual item creation, document upload, Tactiq transcript, video upload, Fathom import). Item payloads and the meeting viewer payload carry `created_by_name` + `created_by_external`; the badge shows next to the author on item rows and in the meeting viewer header. Existing rows and platform ingestion have no author.
- **Team page:** externals are listed with the badge; their role cannot be changed and they cannot be (un)assigned there (409 `external_member`, managed per project; converting to a regular member is out of scope). Removing them from the team page removes them from every project of A (12.5 behaviour).
- **"Project's people list":** there is no project people list in the UI today; the "Pessoas externas" section is that list (with the badge). The project detail API also flags `is_external` on `members`. External people themselves see no member list of A (empty `members`).
- **Removal:** removing an external from a project deletes their external-only `project_members` row (`role = 'external'`), or only clears `access` when the row is also an assignment made by a shared organization they belong to; when no external grant is left on A's projects, the `external` membership is deleted. Content stays.
- **Drive folders (12.9):** folders shared by an external person are not offered as A's folders (`drive_folders._member_emails` ignores `external`).
- **Switcher:** A appears with role `external`; the option reads "A (Externo)" and the badge shows next to the switcher when A is active.

### Security review fixes (2026-10-15)
- **HIGH — the external row was an "assignment":** a `project_members` row created by a project invitation could satisfy "assigned member of a shared organization" (a plain member U of a shared organization S got S's level, S's internal meetings, S's admin could not unassign, and A's removal left U with access). Fix: the row records its origin. External-only rows have `project_members.role = 'external'`; every "assigned" check in `access.py` (`_assigned_project_ids`, `_is_assigned`) and the team page's assignment list ignore them. S's admin assigning U over such a row sets `role = 'member'` and keeps `access`; S unassigning (or removing U from S) turns it back into an external-only row (`release_assignment`). A removing the external access deletes an external-only row, or only clears `access` on a row that is also S's assignment (`release_external_grant`). `_other_granting_organizations` no longer counts external grants, so they never block S's admin. Tests cover both orders (invite then share, share then invite), S assign/unassign, S removing U, and A's removal → 403.
- **MEDIUM — ingestion queue:** `GET /api/ingestion`, `/ingestion/history` and `/ingestion/count` show a project reached **only** as an external person (`access.guest_project_ids`) with meetings past review only (`approved` / `processed`), and drop `approved_by_name`, `rejected_by_name`, `extraction_error` and `job` from those rows. Shared organizations keep the previous behaviour (applying it to them would hide their own pending meetings: not a one-line consistent change). A user who is external on a project **and** reaches it through an organization is not restricted.
- **LOW:** Google sign-in from a project invitation checks `already_member` (409, new `google.errors.already_member` message) before binding the Google account; two concurrent first accepts of the same person (two projects) answer 409 instead of 500 (IntegrityError caught in `invitations._join`).

### Gaps / follow-ups
- Documented, not fixed (security review, low): item/meeting payloads expose `created_by_name` to everyone who can see the item (also shared organizations); the platform page's member counts include external people; an organization invitation (12.5) for someone who became external meanwhile is a no-op on accept (they stay external); the "one live invitation per e-mail + project" rule is enforced in code, not by a unique index.
- Decided by Rami (2026-10-15): contributor items are reviewed (pending) and authors see their own contributions (decisions 7, 8).
- E-mail sending (12.14). The e-mail sender stub already receives a project-specific subject/body.

## Out of scope (phase 2+)

- Sending the invite by e-mail (12.14 Resend).
- Searching organizations by name (dropped: it would expose the client list).
- An external person inviting others.
- Converting an external person into a regular member (remove, then invite through the team page).
